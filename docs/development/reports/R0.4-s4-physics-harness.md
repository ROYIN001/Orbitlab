## What was recorded, and where

Three test files and their references under `tests/eo-phy-6/`. Each number is
written as the 16 hex digits of its Float64 bit pattern. Hex tells +0 from −0,
as `Object.is` does, and never rounds. NaN is written `NaN`, by position,
because its payload is not specified (S07 §07.4 step 4(a)).

| Part | File | What it holds | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | `propagator.test.ts` | 10 `propagate()` cases. For each: SHA-256 of every sample's 8 fields + `steps` + `lifetime`; the first and last samples in full; every sample in full when there are 30 or fewer. Also `acceleration()` at z = ±0, at `mass = 0` (NaN, −∞, −∞), and force by force on the equator's axes. | `ref/propagator.json` |
| (b) | `apollo11.test.ts` | Apollo 11 point-mass (the viewer's mission, calm, seed 1) from the S-IVB's TLI ignition (t = 9856.2 s) to `evt.entryInterface` (t = 702185.69 s). SHA-256 of `[t, r, v]` at the first step past each whole second (31,011 samples in 43,790 steps) and, apart, of the event log from the ignition on (45 events: key, t, every param, the params' keys sorted). The 9 targeting results in full hex, taken at the call (so a reused scratch result cannot alias): `targetMidcourse` ×1, `csiImpulse` ×1, `cdhImpulse` ×1, `interceptImpulse` ×3, `targetEntry` ×3. | `ref/apollo11.json` |
| (c) | in `propagator.test.ts`, `apollo11.test.ts` | Measures, never gated (a count of 0 where the base had calls is only warned about, since an allocation-free EQ-9 variant may call the forces another way): `deriv` (`acceleration()`) calls per propagator case; the Apollo run's steps and Moon/Sun ephemeris calls. | the same files, `derivCalls` / `measures` |
| (d) | `msis-order.test.ts` | `msisDensity` at 12 points (0 to 2000 km, every branch of the model) and `airDensity` at 4 ECI points: the same, `Object.is`, after 5 different call histories, and equal to the recorded hex. A self-test proves the check catches a double that leaks one call into the next. | `ref/msis.json` |

What these show today:

- **±0.** The two equatorial runs give the same samples bit for bit. A sign of zero does not show in the elements (`elementsOf` takes the `nn ≤ 1e-12` branch: raan 0, argp 0). `acceleration()` does show it. At z = +0 the z component is −0 (k·z with k < 0); at z = −0 it is +0. Both are recorded. Under OR-1 a sign of zero that shows in no output counts as unchanged. These records exist so that a sign that *does* show gets caught (EQ-9's preallocated accumulators).
- **Non-finite.** The NaN error is accepted (`err > tol` is false). Then h and t turn NaN and the loop ends by itself after 2 steps, with only the start sample. For `mass = 0` that sample is finite; for the NaN input its elements are NaN. The run returns `lifetime: null` and no error. This is pinned, not approved: making it an explicit error is a separate bug-fix PR, not an EQ change.
- **(d) passes.** No call history changes any density by a bit, through `msisDensity` or through density.ts's shared scratch input. That holds after `gtd7` with the diurnal switches off, which leaves `prepare`'s local-time terms from the call before, and after `gtd7` in CGS units. So §07.4 step 4's fallback ("if (d) fails, FSAL and the reuse of a rejected step's first stage leave EQ-9") is **not** triggered: EQ-9 may keep both.
- **(c) baseline for KPI-11.** Cowell calls `deriv` exactly 7 times a step; it calls it more only when a step is rejected. EQ-9's FSAL would make it 6 a step plus 1. The Apollo run evaluates the Moon's ephemeris 2,309,319 times and the Sun's 1,770,236 times in 43,790 steps. That is EQ-11's baseline (S09: ephemerides recomputed at the same jd).

## Sabotage: the harness bites

Each sabotage was a one-point edit to `src/` in a throwaway worktree of `007b039`, never committed. It was run against `tests/eo-phy-6`, and `src/` was restored after each one (`git status -- src` clean). The unmodified tree passes 18/18. All 11 were run on the first commit and again on the final one (after the review fixes and the re-recording), with the same failure counts.

| # | Sabotage (one point) | §07.5 asks | Fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | `propagate.ts`: the 5th-order sum `B5.reduce` → `reduceRight` (summation order) | (a) | 7 of 18: every Cowell case whose steps are accepted |
| S2 | `forces.ts`: Moon added before Sun (call and summation order) | (a) | 3: `leo400-all-30d`, `gto-sun-moon-srp-10d`, `eccentric-untilDown` |
| S3 | `forces.ts`: `J3_EARTH` +1 ulp | — | 2: `acceleration()` at ±0, force by force |
| S3b | `forces.ts`: `P_SUN` +1 ulp | — | 1: force by force (see the note below) |
| S3c | `forces.ts`: `MU_SUN` +1 ulp | — | 4: the 3 cases with the Sun + force by force |
| S4 | `forces.ts`: the sign of a zero z component of `acceleration()` flipped | 0° case | 2: `acceleration()` at ±0, force by force |
| S5 | `propagate.ts`: `steps++` only when `err` is finite | non-finite | 2: `nonfinite-mass0`, `nonfinite-nan-input` |
| S6 | `lunar/cislunar.ts`: near the Moon, Moon + (Sun + Moon's J2/C22) instead of (Moon + Sun) + J2/C22 (summation order) | (b) | 1: Apollo 11 |
| S7 | `lunar/ephemeris.ts`: the Moon's `MU_MOON` +1 ulp | — | 1: Apollo 11 |
| S8 | `msis.ts`: `msisDensity` keeps a call count and returns every second answer 1 ulp up (leaked state) | (d) | 5: both (d) checks, `mean-leo300-drag-2y`, and both `acceleration()` records |

Also inside the suite: (d)'s self-test "the check catches a double that leaks one call into the next" (§07.5's "leaked state in a test double") passes on the base. Its leaky double is flagged.

**Why S3b needed force-by-force records.** The first version of the harness did not catch S3b. Sunlight pressure on this spacecraft is about 10⁻⁸ m/s². A 1-ulp change to it is far below the last bit of gravity's 8.7 m/s², so no trajectory moves. The output really is identical, and under OR-1 that is no change. A future EQ change could still alter a small force's last bit in a way that does show elsewhere. So the harness also records `acceleration()` with one force at a time, at four points on the equator's axes. There the central term's and J2's two other components are exactly ±0, so each force shows at its own resolution. With that record S3b fails. S3 (J3) is caught the same way, and S1–S2 and S3c move trajectories directly.

The sabotage script (Python, with each edit as an exact string replacement) was kept outside the repo. The edits are listed above in full, so each run can be repeated by hand.

## Second-agent review (physics)

A second agent reviewed the first commit (`c0c441b`). It read the code and the fixtures and ran the suite (18/18 on Node 22.23.3). Its verdict was **changes requested**: one blocking finding and seven nits. Every finding is now fixed. The references were then re-recorded on `5f9aa2e`, twice, with byte-identical results. Nothing in `src/` changed between the two recordings; the second one is needed only because the sampling rule (nit 4) and the key sorting (nit 7) changed what is hashed.

It confirmed:
- `hex()` is big-endian on both sides, so equal hex is exactly `Object.is`.
- The `untilDown` case really comes down.
- Tolerance 1e-12 forces exactly one rejection (46,053 = 7 × 6,578 + 7).
- The 0° case stays in its plane, through `elementsOf`'s `nn ≤ 1e-12` branch.
- The non-finite cases end as described.
- The `vi.mock` wrappers pass results through unchanged and reach their callers.
- The Apollo run is deterministic: fixed launch time, seed 1, no `Math.random`.

| # | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 **blocking** | (c) asserted `derivCalls ≥ steps`. An allocation-free EQ-9 variant that calls the forces some other way would count 0 here and fail an identical-output change. | The assertion is gone. (c) only reports the counts, and it warns when a count drops to 0. |
| 2 | The ±0 samples hash alike; only `acceleration()` holds the sign. | The header says so plainly (the sums restart from +0). |
| 3 | For `mass = 0`, the non-finite force itself was not recorded. | `acceleration()` at `mass = 0` is recorded: `[NaN, −∞, −∞]`. |
| 4 | Apollo sampling fell behind the clock, so it took nearly every step and not one sample a second. | Sampling now waits for the next whole second (`while (next <= t) next += 1`): 31,011 samples where there were 42,373. |
| 5 | Targeting results were held by reference and converted to hex at the end. A reused scratch vector would alias. | Each result is converted to hex at the call. |
| 6 | (c) failed if run alone, and the `onProgress` array could grow on a retry. | `cases()` is rebuilt for each test, and (c) returns early when nothing ran. |
| 7 | Reordering the keys of a `params` literal would change `eventDigest`. | Params keys are sorted before hashing. |
| 8 | The leaky double was clean only at index 0. | It now carries the previous call's index into its answer. |

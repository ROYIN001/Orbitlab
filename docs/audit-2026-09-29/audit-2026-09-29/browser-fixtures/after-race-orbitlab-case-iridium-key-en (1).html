Answer key — Case sheet: Iridium 33 and Cosmos 2251

The first collision of two whole satellites, 10 February 2009

Name:Code:CASE-IRIDIUM

Questions

  1. 226 m (± 5 m)

    |r₂ − r₁|: the difference of the two positions, component by component, then its length.

  2. 11.65 km/s (± 0.05 km/s)

    |v₂ − v₁|. The velocities are Earth-fixed; the Earth's turning adds ω × r to each, nearly the same for both 226 m apart, so it drops out of the difference.

  3. 102 ° (± 2 °)

    cos θ = v₁ · v₂ / (|v₁| |v₂|), with each velocity as seen from space (v + ω × r): nearly square across.

  4. 19.9 m (± 0.1 m)

    The sum of the two hard-body radii.

  5. 18.0 m (± 0.9 m)

    σ² = σ₁² cos² θ + σ₂² sin² θ, θ the σ₁ axis's angle from the miss.

  6. 13 (± 1)

    The miss divided by σ: some 13. A Gaussian that many standard deviations out is vanishingly thin, hence the message's 10⁻⁵¹, which says more about the uncertainties it was given than about the satellites.

  7. c) The uncertainties in the message were far smaller than the real errors of the orbits.
  8. 323 (± 30)

    This app's probability for that case, 0.032 (the paper's: 0.033), divided by 10⁻⁴.

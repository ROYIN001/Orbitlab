### B4 · MAJOR · physics

**state.maxQ is overwritten by re-entry dynamic pressure and the structural-limit check runs exactly once — reproduced live at 1825 kPa with no breakup**

Files: src/physics/simulation.ts

Merges three separately-filed findings (physics-core and two guidance-lens ones). Still live at simulation.ts:583 and :597-600. (1) `if (s.status === 'ascent' && alt < 100e3 && q > s.maxQ.value) s.maxQ = {...}` is guarded only by status, and a vehicle that lofts and falls back is still 'ascent', so ballistic re-entry q replaces the true ascent peak in the field the HUD, telemetry and every captured frame read. (2) The structural test `if (s.maxQ.value > this.maxQAscent * 1.15)` lives INSIDE the one-shot `!events.some('evt.maxQ')` peak-detection block, so it is evaluated at most once and can also never run at all when q is still rising at impact. Verified today on a default-guidance flight: protonm + 11 500 kg → leo from Baikonur records maxQ 1825 kPa, apexes at 187.7 km and impacts at T+1099 s with no evt.structuralFailure anywhere in the event list, against a spec placard of tens of kPa. Verifiers also refuted the claim that this corrupts the tuner: autotune.ts gates `reason='maxQ'` on `success`, which a fall-back run can never satisfy, so the misclassification path is much narrower than filed. Consequences are a nonsense Max-Q readout, inflated losses.drag, and a missing or badly mistimed structural-failure and Max-Q marker on the event bar the user wants.

Fix: Three separate changes, in this order. (a) Latch the ascent peak: stop updating s.maxQ once vz has been negative below 100 km for a sustained interval (prefer this to a bare vz > 0 test, which trips on flat trajectories and hot-staging transients) or record it in a distinct ascentMaxQ field. (b) Move the placard test out of the one-shot block and evaluate instantaneous q against maxQAscent * 1.15 on every step — but NOT in stepOrbit and not after payload separation, where orbitArea() models a small satellite and a stacked launcher's placard is meaningless. (c) Decouple Max-Q event detection from the structural check, and detect the peak only while climbing / before MECO, or the event bar keeps getting a wrong Max-Q time. Expect this to newly destroy vehicles that today survive between 1.15× placard and ground impact — that is the documented behaviour but it will move fleet rows, so land it with B2's triage.

### B5 · MAJOR · guidance-mission

**evt.targetOrbit is emitted with no comparison of the achieved orbit to the target — the HUD says 'Target orbit achieved' on orbits that are not**

Files: src/physics/simulation.ts, src/physics/mission.ts

Still live: reachTargetOrbit(el, onTarget = true) at simulation.ts:1122 is called from four sites and only the finishBurnIncomplete path (:1115) passes false. There is no residual check. Both verifiers narrowed the mechanism and struck the auditor's evidence matrix (556 × 1841, 421 × 1639, 500 × 919, 599 × 1315 etc. do not reproduce), so use their replacement evidence: an implicit check does exist, because every cut-off calls replanRemainingBurns → replanBurns → planBurns, which re-derives the outstanding burns from the ACHIEVED orbit, so the burn list normally empties only once the residuals fall under planBurns' own thresholds. The defect is that those thresholds become the de-facto acceptance criterion and they are loose, one-sided and capped: apoapsis tolerance is max(APOAPSIS_TOLERANCE 8 km, 1.8 % of target apogee) — 644 km of slop at GTO; the perigee test `target.perigee > hIns + tol` is ONE-SIDED so a perigee above target is never corrected; the inclination gate is 0.05° but the resulting burn is then dropped by `.filter(b => b.dvEstimate > 2)`; and MAX_REPLANS = 6 (simulation.ts:158) freezes the plan, after which whatever is left is declared success. Reproducible false successes, all emitting evt.targetOrbit / severity 'success': every GTO mission via the loose apogee band plus the one-sided perigee test (soyuz21b 332 × 35717, protonm 345 × 35737, falcon9 269 × 35721, pslvxl 274 × 35715 against a 250 × 35786 target — perigee up to 95 km high); electron/iss and electron/polar at replans = 6; falcon9/geo at i = 0.25° against a 0° target.

Fix: Add an explicit acceptance test in reachTargetOrbit, but evaluate it on MEAN elements, not instantaneous osculating ones — osculating apsides swing 14–31 km per revolution under J2 in this model and any gate below that is noise. Size tolerances per parameter and never tighter than what the planner will correct: apogee max(15 km, 2 %), perigee max(15 km, 2 %), inclination 0.15° for LEO/GTO but an LTAN-drift criterion (~0.03°) for SSO. Reuse the bands already in tests/fleet-defaults.test.ts (max(10 km, 2 %), 0.3°) rather than inventing new ones. Include RAAN when plan.target.raan is non-null — a 420 km circular orbit in the wrong plane is a failed ISS mission. Emit evt.offTargetOrbit with the residuals otherwise. Fix the two root causes in the same change or the new check just converts silent false successes into unexplained off-target endings: make the perigee test in planBurns two-sided, and replace the hard MAX_REPLANS freeze with a residual-based stop.

### B6 · MAJOR · guidance-mission

**Post-insertion apsis trim never converges: sub-tolerance burns fire and complete within one step while the periapsis ratchets down, and MAX_REPLANS freezes the plan**

Files: src/physics/simulation.ts, src/physics/mission.ts

This is what survives of the 'burn plan is never re-planned' finding, which both verifiers marked contested because replanBurns()/`lowering`/BURN_PREORIENT_TIME/two-sided completion are all now present. The residual is a convergence defect. Verifier evidence: Starship + 50 t → 500 km and Falcon 9 + 2.2 t → 400 km enter a loop of ~4–5 m/s raiseApoapsis trims; each ignites and reports evt.burnComplete inside one integration step without moving the apoapsis (520→519, 416→416) while the periapsis ratchets DOWN one notch per orbit (500→499→480→478→477→476→474 km; 400→398→378→377→375→374→372 km). The periapsis dropping rather than the apoapsis shows the trim fires near apoapsis, not periapsis — timeToPeriapsis(el) is ill-conditioned at e ≈ 0.0015 where there is no well-defined periapsis. Compounding it, the replan tolerance max(8 km, 1.8 % of target apogee) = 9 km at a 500 km target is tighter than such a trim can deliver, so a 20 km residual keeps re-planning a burn that cannot fix it; after MAX_REPLANS = 6 the sim sits in coast indefinitely. I reproduced the same signature today: vulcan/leo at 50 % payload passes at shipped kick 1.5° but ends `coast` at 596 × 499 km at kick 2.5, 4, 6 and 9; protonm at 25 % payload needs six burns and 7427 s to converge; vulcan at 25 % apexes at 1808 km and converges only at T+8182 s. Also still absent: replanRemainingBurns is not called on the evt.burnPaused path in checkBurn (both pause branches go straight to scheduleNextBurn).

Fix: Add a delta-v deadband so a trim below roughly 10–20 m/s is skipped rather than ignited. Refuse to schedule an apsis trim when el.e is below the ~0.01 threshold at which timeToPeriapsis is meaningful — schedule at the node instead (see B29). Widen the apoapsis tolerance to match achievable trim accuracy, and bound it above so GTO/GEO does not inherit 644 km of slop. Call replanRemainingBurns on the paused path. Do not add a separate lowerApoapsis burn KIND — the existing single-kind + `lowering` flag factoring is correct; raising and lowering an apsis is the same manoeuvre with opposite sign. Also add the missing direction: there is no burn that LOWERS a periapsis (`if (target.perigee > hIns + tol || needPlane)`), and desiredVelocity clamps rP = min(rP, rm), so an insertion whose periapsis overshot can never be trimmed down.

### B7 · MAJOR · scene

**Earth, atmosphere, plume and smoke ShaderMaterials skip tone mapping and output colour-space conversion — the planet is 2.7–16× too dark in the midtones**

Files: src/render/scene.ts, src/render/plume.ts, src/render/smoke.ts

Still live — `grep -rn 'tonemapping_fragment|colorspace_fragment|toneMapped' src/render/*.ts` returns nothing. The renderer is configured with ACESFilmicToneMapping, exposure 1.05 and SRGBColorSpace, and three.js applies both INSIDE each material's fragment shader via the tonemapping_fragment and colorspace_fragment chunks. EARTH_FRAG and ATMO_FRAG end at `gl_FragColor = vec4(col, 1.0);` with neither chunk, so the Earth and atmosphere write raw linear radiance into an sRGB buffer while every MeshStandardMaterial in the same frame is correctly tone-mapped. Both verifiers established the magnitude is UNDERSTATED, not overstated: the auditor's '0.735 / ~30 % darker' drops the tone-mapping step. Running three's own ACES math at exposure 1.05 then the sRGB OETF: linear 0.05 should encode to 0.204 and is written as 0.050 (~22× low); 0.10 → 0.351 vs 0.100; 0.30 → 0.659 vs 0.300; 0.50 → 0.782 vs 0.500; 0.90 → 0.881 vs 0.900 (indistinguishable). A blue-marble ocean pixel (linear 0.02, 0.06, 0.20) should encode to (0.036, 0.237, 0.521) and is written as (0.020, 0.060, 0.200) — crushed shadows, blown highlights, and B/G channel ratio shifting 2.2 → 3.3, i.e. the over-saturation the auditor noticed. Scope is wider than the original filing: plume.ts (gl_FragColor at the core and sheath shaders) and smoke.ts have the identical omission, which matters directly for the 'visually convincing plume' goal.

Fix: DO NOT APPLY THE AUDITOR'S FIX AS WRITTEN — it breaks the build. three.js already injects tonemapping_pars_fragment and colorspace_pars_fragment into prefixFragment for every non-Raw ShaderMaterial (three.module.js:6934-6941), and neither chunk has an include guard, so adding the *pars* includes re-declares `uniform float toneMappingExposure` and redefines ACESFilmicToneMapping/RRTAndODTFit/sRGBTransferOETF in the same translation unit — a GLSL redefinition error, and the Earth and atmosphere vanish. Add ONLY the two body includes, immediately after each gl_FragColor assignment: `#include <tonemapping_fragment>` then `#include <colorspace_fragment>`, and nothing to the pars block. (tonemapping_fragment is a no-op when TONE_MAPPING is undefined, so it is safe; toneMappingExposure is set for every program at three.module.js:18788.) For the atmosphere's additive rim, `atmoMat.toneMapped = false` plus the colourspace include is an equally sound variant. BUDGET A RETUNE: the shader constants were hand-tuned against the uncorrected pipeline, so night-side city lights will lift ~4× and the constant 0.04 night-limb rim term goes to ~0.17 encoded — expect to pull `night * 1.4` and the 0.04 rim floor down afterwards. Upside the auditors missed: the Earth shader has no exposure input at all and currently ignores the per-frame sky.exposure ramp that the rocket and pad already follow; the fix makes the planet track it.

### B8 · MAJOR · scene

**Staging and payload deploy render as interpenetrating cylinders: the renderer re-anchors the stack to y = 0 at every jettison while debris spawns at exactly s.r**

Files: src/render/rocket.ts, src/render/debris.ts, src/physics/simulation.ts, src/main.ts

Merges the stage-separation and payload-deploy findings, which share one root cause. The scene uses a floating origin at s.r and RocketView draws the BASE of the lowest attached stage there; when a stage detaches the loop `continue`s before `y += part.height`, so the next attached stage is re-anchored to y = 0 — the vehicle teleports ~42 m downward into the hardware it just discarded. Meanwhile spawnStageDebris/separatePayload place the debris at `r: clone(s.r)` with the same dir axis, so the 42 m booster mesh occupies local y ∈ [0, 42] around the 15 m second stage. The verifiers corrected the duration and the fix. The co-location window is `ignitionDelay` alone, not sepDelay + ignitionDelay, because stageTo schedules spawnStageDebris INSIDE the sepDelay callback: 4 s on Falcon 9, 10 s on Atlas V Centaur, measured 6.1–6.7 s to full clearance on F9 and 12.3 s on Atlas V, ~21 s when the next stage does not ignite, ~45 s for the payload inside the spent upper stage, and the fairing halves drop 15 m into the second stage at jettison. The auditor's '~20 s later' is refuted — at 10 s the gap is 228 m and at 20 s it is 1273 m, because the upper stage accelerates at ~7.5 m/s² away from the free-falling booster. F9/FH z-fight (equal 3.66 m diameters); every other vehicle simply occludes the narrower upper stage.

Fix: Renderer-only; do not touch simulation.ts. (1) In RocketView.update, stop losing the stacking height of detached parts: accumulate `offset = sum(part.height for detached parts below the first attached one)` and start the layout at y = offset, keeping the `continue` for visibility/plumes. Expose it as RocketView.baseOffset. (2) Feed that offset into src/render/cameras.ts CameraFocus (`desiredTarget = pos + dir*(baseOffset + height*aim)`, lift relative to the same point) or the camera aims at the volume the booster vacated. (3) Leave spawnStageDebris's `r: clone(s.r)` and DebrisView's `body.position.y = L/2` alone — with (1) they are already correct. DO NOT apply the auditor's two sub-fixes together: `body.position.y = -L/2` and `spawn at s.r - dir*length` are alternatives, and both at once double-offsets the booster to axial [-84, -42]. And `-L/2` is WRONG for strap-ons, which share DebrisView.build and are correctly base-aligned — gate any such shift on `d.visual.kind === 'stage'`. (4) Add `baseOffset` to DebrisVisual for the fairing (stack top) and the spent stage at separatePayload, and keep the satellite at the station it occupied rather than moving satG.position.y to height/2. (5) If a separation effect is added, make it per-vehicle: pneumatic pusher + cold-gas flip for Falcon 9/Heavy, nose LOX venting for Soyuz strap-ons, solid retros for Atlas V/H3-class, and nothing at all for hot-staged separations (Soyuz Blok I, Long March, Proton) where the upper engine plume through the lattice interstage IS the effect.

### B9 · MAJOR · scene

**Strap-on booster debris appears ~90° away from where the booster was attached: main.ts and spawnBoosterDebris swap the cos/sin basis vectors**

Files: src/main.ts, src/physics/simulation.ts, src/render/rocket.ts

Both verifiers refuted the auditor's diagnosis and located the real bug, which is a genuine reflection, not the claimed +π/4 phase. There is NO `Math.PI / 4` term in rocket.ts's booster placement and no `xAxis` variable there; the renderer's local frame is built in src/main.ts updateVisuals (~lines 299-311) via THREE.Matrix4.makeBasis(xAxis, dir, side) with `up = normalize(r)`, `side = normalize(cross(dir, up))`, `xAxis = normalize(cross(dir, side))`, assigning basis X = xAxis, Y = dir, Z = side. rocket.ts then places each booster at `position.set(cos(ang)*off, y, sin(ang)*off)`, i.e. cos → xAxis, sin → side. simulation.ts spawnBoosterDebris computes the algebraically identical vectors (`side2 = normalize(cross(along, side))` equals main.ts's xAxis) but builds `lateral = side*cos(ang) + side2*sin(ang)` — the coefficients on side and side2 are swapped between the two files, which is a reflection u(ang) → u(90° − ang), not a rotation. For the 4-booster cardinal ring Soyuz actually uses (0/90/180/270°) this is an exact 90° jump, verified symbolically and by probe; for other counts the per-booster jump is irregular. So the Korolev cross renders as a teleport, and the magnitude is worse than the '45°' in the title.

Fix: One line in src/physics/simulation.ts spawnBoosterDebris: `const lateral = add(scale(side2, Math.cos(ang)), scale(side, Math.sin(ang)));`. Verified by direct substitution to match the renderer's world placement exactly. A shared azimuth helper is unnecessary — the angle formula `(2πk)/count` already matches between the two files once the π/4 claim is dropped; only the basis mapping needs unifying. Separately, note that the `groups.length > 1` phase branch in rocket.ts can never fire because every vehicle in src/data/vehicles.ts supplies a single-element boosters array. Once aligned, add the Korolev-cross motion the finding asks for: a small tangential velocity plus a tip-out angular rate so the conical tops splay outward.

### B14 · MAJOR · physics

**Steering and drag losses are measured against the air-relative velocity, so the reported Δv budget does not close (~485 m/s missing on a Soyuz ascent)**

Files: src/physics/simulation.ts

Still live at simulation.ts:572-578: `const refDir = alt < 100e3 && vAirMag > 1 ? scale(vAir, 1/vAirMag) : ...; s.losses.steering += thrustAccel * (1 - cosA) * dt;` and `s.losses.drag += (q * cd * area) / mass * dt`. This corrupts only the telemetry Δv/loss panel and docs/PHYSICS.md §4's promise, never the flown trajectory.

Fix: Use the inertial velocity unit vector as refDir at all altitudes, and accumulate drag as (D/m)·max(0, v̂_air·v̂) dt. Add a test asserting dvThrust − (Δ|v| + gravity + drag + steering) stays inside a FEW m/s, not the auditor's 1 m/s — that tolerance is unachievable without also improving the quadrature (trapezoidal, or accumulate inside the RK4 stages). ### B40 · MINOR · physics

**Physics minor cluster: g-load, ground reference, Earth radius, coast propagation, Max-Q event timing, circular-orbit apsis scheduling, hyperbolic Kepler, liftoff detection**

Nine verified minors, kept together because each is a few lines. (1) G-LOAD: `s.gLoad = Math.hypot(thrustAccel, dragAccel)/G0` is only right if the two are perpendicular, which they never are — during ascent they are near anti-parallel, so hypot(18, 3.2)/G0 = 1.864 g against the truth (18−3.2)/G0 = 1.509 g, a 23 % overestimate; stepOrbit forces gLoad = 0 even while applying drag below 1000 km, and prelaunch leaves the initialised 1. ## Gaps not covered

GAPS — subjects no auditor covered, ordered by how much they could hurt.

1. NUMERICAL INTEGRATOR QUALITY AND EVENT-TIME GRANULARITY. Six auditors examined what the physics computes and nobody examined how well it is integrated. There is no energy- or momentum-conservation study over long propagation, no step-size convergence study for the RK4 path, and no validation of the suggestedDt() policy (which drops to 0.25 s in ascent above 100 km and rises to 30 s in coast). This matters directly for the user's first goal: every event lands on a step boundary, so evt.meco, evt.stageSep and evt.seco carry up to a full step of quantisation — on the event bar that is fine in ascent and potentially tens of seconds in coast. Nobody proposed bisecting the step to the event condition. One auditor's loss-budget residual (B14) is partly quadrature error, which is the same gap seen from another angle.

2. GMST / SIDEREAL TIME / ECI↔GEODETIC CORRECTNESS. The launch-window feature, the ground track, the 2D map, the terminator and the whole RAAN-targeting story all rest on the epoch handling and the ECI-to-lat/lon conversion, and nobody verified any of it against an external reference. One duplicate-window bug was found (launchWindows returning near-identical LTAN windows and never finding the second daily opportunity, with tests/mission.test.ts:146 titled 'exist twice per day' while only asserting length === 2, so it passes on duplicates) — but that is a search-loop bug, not a check of the underlying astronomy. If GMST or the epoch is off, every window, every ground track and every RAAN comparison is off together and nothing in the suite would notice.

3. FAILURE INJECTION AND BOOSTER RECOVERY WERE NEVER EXERCISED. Nobody flew engineOut, thrustLoss, prematureSep or fairingStuck for physical plausibility, nor the boosterRecovery path end to end. The recovery model was touched only tangentially: the dead Debris.cd (B15), the hardcoded 9 m/s² in the suicide-burn reference (B39), and the missing fins/legs on debris meshes (B41). Nobody validated the recovery propellant reserve, the boostback/entry/landing burn sequence, landing accuracy, or droneship vs RTLS. There is no aeroheating or entry-heating model at all, and nobody questioned whether that is acceptable for a sim that renders re-entering boosters.

4. THAI LOCALISATION. Only the Russian dictionary was reviewed for terminology. src/i18n/th.ts was never audited for aerospace vocabulary, and Thai is the user's own language. Given that the Russian pass found a mislabelled instrument and two colliding strings in one sitting, the Thai dictionary should get an equivalent pass — plus the character-count truncation issue (B31) bites Thai hardest.

5. CI, DEPLOY AND SUPPLY CHAIN. .github/workflows is in the source map and no auditor reported on it: nobody checked whether CI runs tsc and vitest, whether the Pages deploy sets the correct base path for a project site, whether the texture assets are actually deployed, or what happens when a texture 404s. No npm audit, no dependency review, no CSP. Note that `npx tsc --noEmit` is currently RED on a leftover probe file (B42), so if CI typechecks, it is already failing.

6. IN-BROWSER PERFORMANCE AND GPU MEMORY. Every perf claim in the audit is from static reading or Node timings. Nobody opened the app, measured a frame time with the WebGL scene live, profiled GPU memory across repeated setup edits, or verified the 721 kB bundle's cold-start time. The dispose() wiring landed during the audit and nobody has confirmed it actually reclaims anything.

7. DOCUMENTATION AS A DELIVERABLE. Auditors cited docs/PHYSICS.md only to catch contradictions (§3 structural limit, §4 steering angle, §6 kick stages, §7 nodal regression, §9 decay). Nobody reviewed README.md or docs/PHYSICS.md as documents for accuracy and completeness against the current code, and nobody reconciled docs/ARCHITECTURE-PLAN.md's wave structure with the audit findings — which matters because two verifiers independently discovered that plan mid-review and it changes the correct fix for B3 (finish wave 2, not delete frame.ts).

8. LONG-RUN AND REPEATED-SESSION BEHAVIOUR. debrisCounter and matCache were flagged as shared module state (B42) but nobody soak-tested repeated launch/reset cycles for state leakage between Simulation instances, nor ran a mission for hours at high warp to see what actually degrades first.

9. AUDIO. There is none, and nobody mentioned it. Not a defect, but for a sim whose goals are "visually convincing pad/rocket/plume/separation" and a timeline that matches published launches, engine ignition, staging and callouts are a large part of what makes a launch read as real. Worth a decision rather than an omission.

10. CROSS-WAVE CONFLICT — a process gap, and the most urgent of these. Six lenses audited a moving target: src/physics/* and src/data/vehicles.ts changed four times during the review, three verifiers independently reported line numbers shifting under them, one verifier hit `applyVehicleGuidanceDefaults is not defined` mid-probe, another found `npx tsc --noEmit` transiently failing with four errors in simulation.ts and main.ts, and one deleted another agent's probe files by accident. tests/wave1g/ (9 files) and tests/zzprobe-lensb.test.ts are still in the repo and one of them is breaking the typecheck right now. Nobody was assigned to check that the six fix waves do not conflict with each other — and B1 is exactly that failure mode: a guidance fix wave repaired the chief's two reported cases and broke the app's own default mission, with the acceptance gate excluding the row that would have caught it. Recommend a single integrator, a frozen revision per audit round, and a rule that the fleet gate may not add an EXCLUDED entry without a signed-off physical justification.

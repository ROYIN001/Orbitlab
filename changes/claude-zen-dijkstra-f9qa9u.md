## CHANGELOG

- Replay (bug fix): a spent stage falling through the air while the vehicle coasts, docks or waits is recorded every 5 s, so a replay draws it where it was, not just near its path. While the vehicle coasted, the recorder kept a frame every 10–30 s, and one span could hold a body's whole drag pulse: measured every second against the six-DOF simulation over the 20 watch missions, Apollo 11's S-II was drawn up to 3.4 km from where it was, Sputnik's boosters 2.3 km, Soyuz MS-16's Blok A 0.8 km and the H-IIA first stage 0.7 km; now 6, 21, 11 and 24 m, for 0–78 frames more a mission (511 in all, 10.5 MB). Point-mass flights keep every step of the fall where they kept every other (Sputnik 1.6 km → 0.39 km, H-IIA 620 → 51 m). Thinning a long recording no longer opens a gap wider than 5 s where a body falls: at an 800-frame budget the H-IIA stage went from 67.8 km off to 51 m, and a two-day Soyuz MS-25 flight from 33 km to 33 m. Six-DOF Vostok-1 records 4,804 frames, 100.7 MB (was 4,726 and 98.8 MB). Sizes stay under their ceilings.
